Echo: Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-27

Echo is a private podcast player for one household's own use. It is not offered to the public.

What Google Sign-In shares

  • The Google account's name and email address, used only to identify the account that owns the library.
  • If you turn on Google Drive Backup: access to the files Echo itself makes in your Google Drive, and nothing else there. Echo cannot see your other Drive files, Gmail or contacts.

What Echo keeps

  • Your library: the shows you follow, their episodes, your Queue, playlists, bookmarks, listening progress and history, listening stats and your settings. It is stored on your phone.
  • If you use Echo Sync, the same library is kept in a private cloud database, where row-level security limits each row to its owner's account, so it follows you to another phone.
  • If you use Google Drive Backup, a copy of your library is saved to a folder Echo makes in your own Google Drive.
  • Feedback you send from the app, with the details you choose to include.

Who else is involved

  • Podcast episodes and artwork are downloaded straight from each show's own host, as with any podcast app.
  • Podcast search and Discover go through the developer's own server, which asks Podcast Index on Echo's behalf. Only the search words are sent, never your account.
  • If paid plans are offered, purchases are handled by Google Play, and RevenueCat records which plan the account has. Echo never sees card details.

What it doesn't do

Nothing is sold or used for advertising. There are no ads, analytics or crash-reporting tools, and no location tracking.

Your choices

  • Everything can be exported or deleted in the app, under Settings → Data, including deleting the account. You can also ask for deletion on the account and data deletion page.
  • To remove Echo's access to your Google account: Google Account → Security → Third-party access → Echo → Remove access.

Contact

Use the developer email shown on Google's sign-in screen for Echo.
